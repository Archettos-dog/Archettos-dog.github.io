**MEMORANDUM**

**DATE:** June 16, 2026 

**TO:** Dr. Cheng, MIC Organizing Committee 

**FROM:** AYIES, International School (Class 2122) 

**SUBJECT:** Suggestions on Paper Writing Schedule, Plagiarism Checking, and Attendance for MIC 2026

------

**INTRODUCTORY SUMMARY**

Having participated in the 21st Model International Conference (MIC) this year, I have gained firsthand experience of the entire process, from paper writing to the final conference presentation. The event was well organized and academically rewarding. However, during my participation I noticed several areas where small adjustments could meaningfully improve the experience for future students. This memo presents three suggestions concerning the paper writing and revision timeline, the plagiarism-checking mechanism, and conference attendance. My hope is that these recommendations will help make MIC even more effective in the coming years.

**SUGGESTION 1: ADJUST THE PAPER WRITING AND REVISION TIMELINE**

The current schedule places paper writing and revision very close to the final examination period, which creates considerable pressure for students who must balance MIC work with reviewing for other courses. I would like to suggest the following improvements:

1. Move the first draft deadline earlier in the term, ideally before Week 13, so that students have adequate time to research, write, and reflect.
2. Build in at least two rounds of feedback between students and instructors, with about one week between each round, so that revisions can be made thoughtfully rather than rushed.
3. Provide a clear written timeline at the start of the course, listing every milestone (topic selection, abstract, first draft, revision, final submission) so that students can plan their workload in advance.

A more spread-out schedule would reduce last-minute stress and, more importantly, raise the overall academic quality of the papers submitted.

**SUGGESTION 2: CLARIFY AND STRENGTHEN THE PLAGIARISM-CHECKING MECHANISM**

Academic integrity is central to MIC, and the use of CNKI for plagiarism detection is a sound practice. However, the process could be made more transparent and instructive:

1. Explain the checking standards clearly at the beginning, including the acceptable similarity threshold and which parts of the paper (such as references and common phrases) are excluded from the calculation.
2. Allow students one self-check opportunity before the final submission, so that they can identify and correct unintentional similarity issues themselves.
3. Offer a short tutorial or guideline on proper citation and paraphrasing, since many similarity problems come from inexperience rather than dishonesty.

These measures would help students treat the plagiarism check as a learning tool rather than merely a pass-or-fail gate.

**SUGGESTION 3: IMPROVE CONFERENCE ATTENDANCE**

Strong attendance creates a livelier academic atmosphere and benefits both presenters and the audience. To encourage participation, I suggest:

1. Introduce a modest participation component into the course assessment, rewarding students who attend and engage with questions.
2. Announce the conference theme, schedule, and outstanding student papers in advance through the official WeChat account to build interest beforehand.
3. Combine offline attendance with live streaming in branch venues, while encouraging interaction so that remote attendance does not become passive.

**CONCLUSION**

In summary, I believe MIC can be further strengthened by spreading out the writing timeline, making the plagiarism-checking process more transparent and educational, and actively encouraging attendance. These adjustments would reduce student stress, uphold academic integrity, and foster a more engaged conference community.

I would be glad to discuss any of these suggestions in more detail and to help with their implementation if that would be useful. Thank you for considering my recommendations.
